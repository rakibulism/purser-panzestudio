# Purser: concept brief

A ship's purser holds the money and keeps the crew's accounts. Here the crew is a company's AI agents.

## The problem

Agents now spend money and take actions on their own, and most companies find out afterwards.

- 46.9% of enterprises ran over budget on AI; 5.6% came in under ([Futurum, September 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/enterprise-ai-overruns-hit-46-9-is-the-reckoning-in-fy2027/); total sample not stated on the page).
- 53% of 200 CFOs at companies with $1B or more in revenue mainly review AI usage costs after the fact, and 74% take several business days to several weeks to pause or shut down usage ([Deloitte CFO Signals, Q3 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/insights/topics/business-strategy-growth/3q-2026-cfo-signals-survey.html)).
- One company's agent used a full year's AI compute budget in a weekend, running in a loop ([Fortune, June 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/24/exclusive-vinod-khosla-felicis-runlayer-nanit-30-million-enterprise-ai/)).

## Why it is in the portfolio

- **Enterprise buyers** see their own problem: budgets, approvals, accountability, dense data.
- **Startup buyers** in this space are funded: Runlayer raised $30M ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/24/exclusive-vinod-khosla-felicis-runlayer-nanit-30-million-enterprise-ai/)) and Sapiom raised $35M ([PYMNTS, August 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=4044998)) in 2026.
- It shows judgment, not only polish: forecasting, risky decisions, trust in automation.

## Who opens it

The person who answers for AI spend at a 1,400-person insurer: finance on paper, half platform in practice. They open it every morning with three questions.

1. Will we stay inside this month's money?
2. What is waiting for my decision?
3. Which agents are worth what they cost?

## The Overview screen

One screen first. It is complete before any second page exists.

1. **Where the month lands.** A sentence with the answer, the spend so far, and the day the money runs out at this pace.
2. **Set the limit.** The signature interaction: drag the limit on the spend chart and the forecast redraws, naming the agents that would stop first and the work left undone.
3. **Waiting on you.** Agents asking to go past their authority. Each request says what it wants, why, and what happens if you say no. Allow once, allow up to an amount, or refuse.
4. **Caught overnight.** One agent looped and was stopped. What it was doing, when it was caught, what it cost, and a choice to resume or retire it.
5. **Worth it.** Cost per finished job for each agent, beside what that job cost before. Some agents lose, and the screen says so.
6. **The ledger.** Spend by team and agent, traceable to a single run.

## Rules for the build

- All data is invented, and every total adds up from the rows beneath it.
- Invented company names only. No real vendor's brand or layout.
- US English. Flat surfaces, no shadows or glows. The app runs edge to edge.
- Agents never get a human face. People do.
- Sections lead with plain words about what happened. No stock widget kits.
- The phone is its own native-feeling design.
- Check every width from 375 to 1440, including 613, 768 and 860.
